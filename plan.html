DP1 plan — built from the syllabus audit + ISL's deadline calendar

The one-line plan: DP1 = foundational mastery + habit-building, timed around ISL's own deadlines — exams in January, EE launched in February (proposal 2 March), TOK exhibition planned in March and finished by 16 June, Biology IA experiment in June. The checklists go green ahead of each school date, not after it.

Term 1 · Sep–Dec 2026 — foundations (now)

  • Done: Collaborative Science Project introduction + research/planning (Sep 1 & 24), CAS initial interview (late Sep) — logged, nothing to do.
  • Ongoing (the core loop): when a unit closes in any subject — work its checklist rows to green the same week; run two 20-question quiz sessions per week on that unit; log every lost mark in the error log.
  • Ongoing (cheap, compounding): command terms weekly (reference page) · IA idea log entry whenever a lab sparks a question · one francophone text analysed per week for French.
  • 8 December: English A Lit HL practice commentary — the first real Paper-1 rehearsal: timed, closed-book, two-passage format.
  • December holiday: light, spaced — quiz sessions on amber/red topics only, no cramming.

Term 2 · Jan–Mar 2027 — first exams, then EE launch

  • 12–22 January — First Semester Examinations. From 12 January the school switches to revision-only homework: use that window properly — full timed mocks for math/sciences from the practice page protocol, error-log review sessions the night before each paper.
  • After exams (same week): audit the semester — every red checklist row gets a re-teach; quiz scores from the log show exactly which topics decayed.
  • 17 February: EE launch (half-day). Walk in with the idea log already shortlisted in the EE workspace — subject choice becomes obvious instead of panicked.
  • 2 March — EE proposal due (subject + topic + RRS + initial proposal). One page, but it sets two years: pick something with data you can actually reach. The proposal checklist maps each item to the new-guide criteria.
  • 12 March: EE Reflective Session 1. 23 March: EE equipment order — if the EE or next year's IA needs materials, this is the moment to ask.
  • 25 March: TOK Exhibition plan. 26 March: French A essay draft (HL class — confirm if it applies to her group).

Term 3 · Apr–Jun 2027 — everything lands at once, so stagger it

  • Late April: EE Phase 2 submission (27th) · 28 April: TOK Exhibition draft — aim to have the final version 90% ready here, because May is exams.
  • 13 May: revision-only window opens → late May: Second Semester Examinations. Same mock protocol as January.
  • 7 June: CAS portfolio checkpoint — evidence should be a standing habit, not a June scramble.
  • 7 June: CAS portfolio checkpoint · 9 June: EE conference · 16 June: TOK Exhibition final · late June: Biology IA experiments — the research question should be fixed by mid-May so labwork is routine (DP1 practice sessions in Nov and Jan already walked the criteria).
  • 11 May / 18 June: English A Lit HL Essay (draft → final) — the line of inquiry should be fixed by March so the draft rides on the exam-revision period, not against it. DP1 closes with every checklist ≥ amber, ideally green on the DP2-heavy topics.

What to ask the school for — the legal sourcing route

Teachers hold everything licensed: the Questionbank, full paper archives, guides, TSMs and marked IA samples. One email closes most of the gaps in this library. Send it per subject or to the DP coordinator:

“Could we please receive, for home practice: (1) topical Questionbank sets for the units my daughter's class covers this term; (2) the May 2025 / Nov 2025 / May 2026 papers and markschemes for Biology and Chemistry HL, and recent Math AA SL papers (TZ2); (3) the new History guide and its first-assessment-2028 specimen set; (4) the Language A: Language & Literature 2026-refresh guide and specimen, plus a few past French Paper 1 texts for analysis practice; (5) the sciences IA marked samples from the TSM; (6) the Extended Essay guide (first assessment 2027) and the reflection form. Also: which revision platforms does the school license (e.g. Kognity), and which sixth-subject option would you recommend for her profile?”

  • Why this route: it is the licensed channel — same documents, no copyright grey zone, and the school versions are guaranteed current-edition.
  • Timing: before the February EE launch (the proposal is due 2 March) and before the January exams (papers for mock practice).
  • Also confirm: which sixth-subject calendar items apply (see the sixth-subject hub), and the December 8 “1a practice commentary” reading.

About the practice-engine wishlist in the notes

The voice note asked for: filtered sessions (built ✓), 20-question format (built ✓), instant MCQ marking (built ✓), markscheme-guided scoring for written answers (built as honest self-marking — an automated NLP marker is a real product, not a static-page feature; treat it as a later upgrade), weak-topic flags + tactics (built ✓, rule-based), and question sourcing.

Sourcing note: the seeded banks are original items written to the syllabus. The engine accepts any JSON bank — the legitimate route to official-style items is school-provided topical sets (ISL teachers can export Questionbank content). AI-predicted questions were the third item on the wishlist; if that ever gets built, the notes' own blind spot applies: it needs a defined methodology mapped to the curriculum before the questions can be trusted.
